## The " **Front Matter** " **:** 

## **Cover Page** 

The official first page containing the full thesis title, the author’s name (authors’ names), the supervisor’s name (supervisors’ names), the degree sought, and the university/faculty seal. 

## **Declaration of Originality** 

A formal, signed statement confirming that the work is the student’s/students’ own and that all external sources have been properly cited. 

## **Abstract** 

A concise (usually 300–500 words) summary of the entire thesis, covering the problem, methodology, key AI/ML findings, and final conclusions. 

## **Acknowledgment** 

A personal section in which the student(s) thank supervisor(s), lab mates, family, and any institutions that provided data or funding. 

## **Dedication** 

(Optional) A brief, personal tribute (often just one sentence) to individuals who inspired the author’s (authors’) academic journey. 

## **Table of Contents** 

(ToC) A hierarchical list of all chapters and sub-sections (up to 3 levels) with corresponding page numbers. 

## **List of Figures** 

A sequential index of all diagrams, system architectures, charts, and UI screenshots included in the text.

## **List of Tables** 

A chronological list of all data tables, requirement matrices, and experimental result comparisons. 

## **List of Algorithms** 

(Crucial for AI/ML) A specific index for pseudocode or logical flows (e.g., "Algorithm 1: Modified Ant Colony Optimisation"). 

## **List of Equations** 

A catalogue of the mathematical models and loss functions used, which is vital for the scientific rigour of a thesis. 

## **List of Abbreviations and Acronyms** 

A quick-reference guide defining short-forms like CNN (Convolutional Neural Network) or MLOps to ensure clarity for all readers. 

## **Glossary** 

A dictionary-style list of technical terms; this is especially important to ensure domain-specific jargon is used correctly. 

## **List of Publications** 

A record of any peer-reviewed papers, posters, or conference presentations that the student/students authored based on this research.

- 1.1 Problem Statement 

- 1.2 Motivation and Significance 

- 1.3 Research Gaps, Questions, and Objectives 

- 1.4 Proposed Solution Overview 

- 1.5 Thesis Organisation 

**Key Success Indicators for Chapter 1 [** The Goal: Establish the "Why" and "What." **]** 

- **KSI 1 – Problem Precision:** Defines a specific, non-trivial problem. Avoids vague 

- goals like "improving healthcare" and instead targets "reducing false-positive diagnostic alerts in high-latency clinical environments." 

- **KSI 2 – The "Measurable" Objective:** Research questions (RQs) are framed such that they can be answered with "Yes/No" or a specific metric. For example: "To what extent does a Hybrid Genetic Algorithm reduce computational overhead compared to standard Backpropagation?" 

- **KSI 3 – Scope Boundary:** Explicitly states what the project _will not_ do. This demonstrates professional maturity and a realistic understanding of project constraints. 

- **KSI 4 – Stakeholder Value:** Clearly identifies who benefits from this software (e.g., 

- end-users, developers, or data scientists) and the specific "pain point" being addressed.

## **Appendix A: AI Ethics & Bias Statement** 

Students must provide a section(s) addressing: 

1. Data Fairness: Analysis of the dataset for demographic or systemic bias. 

2. Mitigation: Steps taken to balance the dataset or adjust the loss function to protect minority classes. 

3. Transparency: Disclosure of the model’s "Black Box" nature and any explainability tools used (e.g., SHAP, LIME). 

**Key Success Indicators for Appendix A [** The Goal: Demonstrate Responsible AI practice. **]** 

- **KSI 1 – Quantified Bias Check:** Provides evidence of testing the model on different demographic or categorical subsets of the data to ensure performance parity. 

- **KSI 2 – Ethical Risk Assessment:** Identifies potential "dual-use" or "misuse" cases 

- for their software and suggests guardrails. 

- **KSI 3 – Explainability (XAI):** Demonstrates an attempt to make the "Black Box" model transparent (e.g., using SHAP values to show which features most influenced a specific prediction).

## **Appendix B: Reproducibility Report** 

A mandatory technical checklist including: 

1. Environment: A requirements.txt or environment.yml file. 

2. Containerization: Submission of a Dockerfile ensuring the environment can be recreated. 

3. Deterministic Seeds: Documentation of all random seeds used in stochastic processes (e.g., random_state=42). 

4. Hardware Specs: Details of the GPU/CPU/RAM used for training. 

**Key Success Indicators for Appendix B [** The Goal: Ensure the science is "Verifiable." **]** 

- **KSI 1 – The "One-Command" Test:** An 'A' grade is given if a reviewer can theoretically run a single script (or docker-compose up) to replicate the environment and training start. 

- **KSI 2 – Hyperparameter Log:** Provides a clear table of all tuned parameters, not just the final winning ones. 

- **KSI 3 – Seed Management:** Explicitly lists the random seeds used for data splitting and weight initialisation to ensure the exact results can be recreated.

## **Appendix C: Generative AI (GenAI) Disclosure** 

1. Tool Inventory: List all LLMs used (e.g., Gemini, Claude, GitHub Copilot). 

2. Usage Scope: Explicitly state if AI was used for code generation, bug fixing, or drafting prose. 

3. Verification: A statement confirming that all AI-generated content was audited, corrected, and verified by the student. 

**Key Success Indicators for Appendix C [** The Goal: Academic Integrity & Human-in-theLoop. **]** 

- **KSI 1 – Tactical Transparency:** Clearly differentiates between AI-generated boilerplate code (e.g., API structures) and student-written logic (e.g., a custom fitness function for an Evolutionary Algorithm). 

- **KSI 2 – Critical Audit Log:** Includes examples of where an LLM provided an incorrect or "hallucinated" answer and how the student corrected it. This proves high-level oversight. 

- **KSI 3 – Proportionate Usage:** Demonstrates that the _intellectual core_ of the thesis (the design, the synthesis, and the evaluation) remains the student's original work.
